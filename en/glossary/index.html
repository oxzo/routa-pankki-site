Banking glossary

Banking language isn’t hard once someone explains it. In this glossary, we do.

A

Account statement
An account statement is a summary of one account's activity over a period. It shows payments, withdrawals and the balance.
Amortisation-free period
An amortisation-free period is a stretch during which you pay only interest on a loan, no instalments. The loan term extends accordingly.
Annual percentage rate
The annual percentage rate rolls a loan's interest and all charges into one figure, so you can compare loans on equal terms.
ASP account
An ASP account is a savings account for young people buying a first home. Regular saving earns the right to an interest-subsidised loan.
ATM
An ATM is a machine where you withdraw cash with a card. Many ATMs also let you deposit money to an account.
Authorisation hold
An authorisation hold is an amount your card reserves on the account during a purchase, before the final charge. The money is unavailable while held.

B

Balanced fund
A balanced fund invests in both shares and interest-bearing assets. One fund gives ready diversification across two asset classes.
Bank guarantee
A bank guarantee is the bank's commitment to pay an agreed sum if a company fails its obligation. It's used in contracts like construction and leases.
Banking credentials
Banking credentials are the personal codes you use to log in to the online bank and to identify yourself in digital services.
Beneficiary
A beneficiary is the person to whom an insurance benefit is paid. In life insurance, for example, they receive it when the insured dies.
BIC
BIC is a code that identifies a bank in international payments. Within SEPA an IBAN alone is usually enough.
Book-entry account
A book-entry account holds shares and other securities electronically. You need one to invest directly in shares.
Branch
A branch is a bank office where you do business face to face. It handles matters that need personal advice or identification.
Business ID
A business ID is the official code that identifies a Finnish company. You need it, for example, when opening a business account.
Business loan
A business loan is a loan granted to a company, for instance for investment or growth. Collateral and terms depend on the company's situation.

C

Cash flow
Cash flow is how much money comes into a company and goes out over a period. Healthy cash flow keeps the company able to pay.
Collateral
Collateral is property a borrower pledges as security for a loan. If the loan isn't repaid, the bank can recover its claim from it.
Combination card
A combination card is a single card with both debit and credit functions. At payment you choose which one to use.
Consumer credit
Consumer credit is an unsecured loan for everyday purchases. The rate is usually higher than a home loan's because there is no collateral.
Contactless payment
Contactless payment is a tap of your card or phone on the terminal. For small purchases no PIN is needed.
Credit card
A credit card lets you pay with the bank's credit and settle later. Carrying a balance past the due date incurs interest.
Credit limit
A credit limit is the most you can use from a credit. On a credit card, for example, it's the agreed ceiling for purchases.
Credit line
A credit line is flexible credit you can draw and repay as needed up to an agreed ceiling. You pay interest only on what you use.
Credit record
Your credit record shows how you have handled your debts. The bank checks it before granting a loan or credit.
Credit transfer
A credit transfer moves money from one account to another using the account number. It's the most common way to pay a bill in Finland.

D

Debit card
A debit card charges purchases straight to your everyday account. You spend only money already in the account — no credit.
Deductible
A deductible is the part of a loss you pay yourself. The insurance covers the loss only above the deductible.
Deposit guarantee
A deposit guarantee protects a depositor's money if the bank fails. It covers up to €100,000 per depositor per bank.
Direct payment
Direct payment is a service where the bank pays a recurring bill automatically on the due date. It suits people who don't use online banking.
Diversification
Diversification means dividing your money across several holdings, so one investment's fall doesn't sink the whole portfolio.
Dividend
A dividend is a share of a company's profit paid out to shareholders. The amount is decided by the annual general meeting.
Down payment
A down payment is the part of a purchase you pay from your own funds. For a home loan the bank always requires some of your own financing.

E

E-invoice
An e-invoice is a bill delivered electronically, straight to your online bank. You approve it in a few clicks, with no details to key in.
Equipment leasing
In equipment leasing a company uses a machine by renting it from a finance company instead of owning it. The cost is split into monthly payments.
Equity fund
An equity fund invests in shares. The expected return is high, but the value can swing sharply. It suits a long investment horizon.
Equity savings account
An equity savings account lets you buy and sell shares without tax on each trade. You pay tax only when you withdraw money.
Estate of a deceased person
An estate of a deceased person is their property and debts, managed jointly by the heirs. The bank needs an estate inventory first.
Euribor
Euribor is the euro-area interbank reference rate that most Finnish home loans are tied to. The most common choice is the 12-month Euribor.
Everyday account
An everyday account is the account your daily money runs through — salary in, bills and card payments out. The money is always available.
Exchange rate
An exchange rate tells how much one currency is worth in another. It changes constantly with the market.

F

Fixed rate
A fixed rate stays the same for an agreed period. Your instalment doesn't change even if market rates rise or fall.
Fixed-income fund
A fixed-income fund invests in interest-bearing assets like government and corporate loans. Its value varies less than an equity fund's.
Fixed-term deposit
A fixed-term deposit ties your money up for an agreed period at a fixed rate. You know the interest in advance.
Fund
A fund pools many investors' money and invests it in a diversified way. A portfolio manager handles the investments for you.
Fund unit
A fund unit is a share of a fund's assets. When you invest in a fund you buy units whose value tracks the fund's value.

G

Guarantee
A guarantee means a guarantor commits to repaying a loan if the borrower cannot. It can serve as collateral for the loan.

H

Home loan
A home loan is borrowed to buy a home, usually secured against the home itself. Terms are long, often 20–25 years.

I

IBAN
IBAN is the international format for an account number. A Finnish IBAN starts with FI and is 18 characters long.
Index fund
An index fund tracks a market index rather than trying to beat it. Its charges are usually low because the portfolio isn't actively managed.
Insurance premium
An insurance premium is the price you pay for insurance. It depends on the risk covered and the extent of the cover.
Interest rate
The interest rate is the price of money – what you pay on a loan or earn on a deposit. A loan rate is usually a reference rate plus a margin.
Investment horizon
Your investment horizon is how long you plan to keep your money invested. A long horizon copes better with swings in value.
Invoice financing
In invoice financing a company gets the money from its sales invoices straight away — the bank advances the amount and collects it from the customer.

K

Key information document
A key information document is a short standard sheet stating a product's risks, costs and expected return. It helps you compare products.

L

Letter of credit
A letter of credit is a bank's commitment to pay the seller once the terms of a deal are met. It is used in foreign trade to build trust.
Loan term
The loan term is the time over which a loan is repaid. A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises the total interest paid.

M

Management fee
A management fee is an annual charge for running the fund. It is taken from the fund's value, so it shows up indirectly in the return.
Margin
The margin is the part of a loan's rate the bank adds on top of the reference rate. It is agreed when the loan is granted and stays fixed.

N

Nominal interest rate
The nominal interest rate is the rate on a loan or deposit without charges. The APR shows the total cost more accurately.

O

Ongoing charges
Ongoing charges express a fund's yearly costs as a single figure. They let you compare the costs of different funds.
Online bank
An online bank is the bank's service on the web, where you manage money yourself — pay bills, follow accounts and apply for a loan.

P

Payment default entry
A payment default entry is a note in your credit record about an unpaid debt. It can make getting a loan or credit harder.
Penalty interest
Penalty interest is a raised rate charged on a bill or loan paid late. It starts to accrue after the due date.
Pension saving
Pension saving means building up money for your own retirement, to top up the statutory pension.
Phishing
Phishing is a scam where a criminal tries to get your banking credentials or card details via a message or a fake website.
Price list
A price list sets out what the bank's services cost. It shows the price of an account, card and other services.
Promissory note
A promissory note is a document that records a debt and its terms. It sets the amount, rate, loan term and repayment.

R

Redemption fee
A redemption fee is a charge you pay when selling fund units. It is taken from the amount redeemed at the time of sale.
Reference number
A reference number is the digit string that identifies a payment and allocates it to the right invoice automatically. Found in the payment details.
Reference rate
The reference rate is the market-driven part of a loan's interest rate. In Finland it is usually Euribor; the bank's margin is added on top.
Repayment instalment
A repayment instalment is the part of a loan payment that reduces the principal. The rest of the payment is interest.
Return
Return is how much an investment earns — the rise in value plus any dividends or interest. It can also be negative.
Risk tolerance
Risk tolerance describes how much swing in an investment's value you can bear — both financially and emotionally.

S

Savings account
A savings account holds money you don't need right away. It pays interest, and you can still withdraw the funds when you need them.
SEPA payment
A SEPA payment is a euro credit transfer within the SEPA area. It moves between countries as easily as a domestic payment.
Strong authentication
Strong authentication confirms your identity with two factors, such as an app and a PIN. It protects access to your online bank.
Student loan
A student loan funds studying and is guaranteed by the state. Repayment usually begins only after graduation.
Subscription fee
A subscription fee is a charge you pay when buying fund units. It is taken from the amount invested at the time of subscription.

V

Variable rate
A variable rate follows the reference rate and changes on review dates. Your instalment can rise or fall during the loan term.

W

Working capital
Working capital is the money a company needs for day-to-day operations — purchases, wages and bills before income comes in.
